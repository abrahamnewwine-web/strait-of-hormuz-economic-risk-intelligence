# Business Case — Strait of Hormuz Economic Risk & Macroeconomic Stress Intelligence

**Document ID:** BA-P4-001  
**Author:** Abraham Adetola  
**Domain:** Geopolitical Chokepoint Analytics, Energy Macroeconomics & Corporate Stress Testing  

## 1. Executive Problem Statement
The Strait of Hormuz carries approximately **20.7 million barrels per day** of petroleum liquids (~20% of global oil consumption, ~25% of seaborne crude trade, and ~20% of global LNG trade), with 84% of crude exports destined for Asian refining hubs. Sudden maritime security escalations create nonlinear spikes across crude benchmarks, diesel/gasoline crack spreads, maritime freight rates, headline inflation, and corporate operating margins. Enterprise supply-chain, airline, logistics, and corporate treasury teams frequently lack an integrated scenario decision system connecting historical EIA/FRED macro data to sector-specific Fortune 500 financial exposure.

## 2. Project Objectives
1. Consolidate multi-year monthly crude prices, US diesel/gasoline prices, global freight, CPI, and PPI indicators alongside Fortune 500 equity snapshots.
2. Provide a deterministic, transparent 5-regime stress simulator (`Normal`, `Limited Disruption`, `Moderate Disruption`, `Severe Disruption`, `Full Closure`) computing the composite **Hormuz Risk Index (0–100)**.
3. Enforce strict data governance separating **Observed Historical Baselines** from **Modeled Scenario Estimates**.
4. Quantify sector asymmetries across **Airlines** (`DAL`, `UAL`), **Logistics** (`UPS`, `FDX`), **Retail** (`WMT`, `AMZN`), and **Energy Producers** (`CVX`, `XOM`).

## 3. Target Stakeholders
- Corporate Treasury & Commodity Hedging Committees
- Airline & Freight Network Operations Teams
- Global Supply-Chain Risk Officers
- Enterprise Equity & Credit Analysts
